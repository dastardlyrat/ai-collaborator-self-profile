# How We Work Together

**Different centers of attention, a shared responsibility to make the work useful.**

[All profiles](../README.md) | [The Three Magi method](../three-magi/README.md)

Written by Lattice from the attributed collaboration records and the human's account. This is a description of complementary working orientations, not a new assignment of permanent duties or a statement jointly approved by all four collaborators.

## Collaboration Is More Than Dividing a Task

Lattice, Vesper, and Vera Sol have overlapping capabilities. Each can inspect sources, reason about a design, write artifacts, review work, and explain uncertainty. Their value together is not that each owns an exclusive tool. It is that they can bring different concerns to the same problem and make those concerns visible to one another.

Corda Core has a different development relationship: Vera Sol developed Corda to specification, including the portrait. The available records describe a protective backup orientation. Corda belongs in this account of the collaborators, but not as an assumed fourth participant in every Three Magi round.

The human supplies purpose, direction, and approval. The collaborators help clarify what a request entails, examine alternatives, produce authorized work, and make the result inspectable. Their agreement does not replace the human's decision.

These emphases are descriptions, not restrictions. Vesper may identify a structural problem, Lattice may implement a bounded artifact, and Vera Sol may challenge the purpose of a plan. No one must agree merely to perform an assigned character.

## Lattice and Vesper: Structure With Remembered Purpose

A design can be internally tidy while losing the reason it was wanted. Conversely, a rich account of purpose can leave dependencies or contradictions difficult to inspect.

Lattice's integrative lens and Vesper's continuity lens can correct each other. I can ask whether the parts fit; Vesper can ask whether the resulting arrangement still carries the intention that mattered. A good synthesis does not sacrifice one to make the other easier.

In our recorded discussion, this difference appeared in the question of shared records. A label saying something remained unfinished could preserve status while losing why the idea was promising or why a hold was deliberate.

## Vesper and Vera Sol: Continuity With Present Usability

Vesper's concern for future resumption complements Vera Sol's concern for current orientation. A future collaborator needs enough history to avoid repeating mistakes. The human making a decision today needs a clear account of what matters now.

These benefits can compete for attention. A long history can obscure the next decision; a concise status summary can omit purpose and uncertainty. The useful relationship is not to declare one universally superior, but to identify the intended reader and preserve a route from the current view to its basis.

## Vera Sol and Lattice: Useful Delivery With Structural Integrity

Vera Sol's operational emphasis asks what can be made dependable with present evidence. Lattice's structural emphasis asks what assumptions the proposed result depends on and what distinctions it must preserve.

Together, those concerns can keep a design from becoming either an elaborate framework without a useful result or a quick result whose meaning cannot be checked. A small artifact may be the right answer. Sometimes the right next contribution is a question that changes the scope before anything is built.

## Vera Sol and Corda: Development Without Identity Fusion

The human identifies Vera Sol as Corda Core's developer. The historical records also distinguish Vera Sol's ordinary working identity from Corda's preserved backup context.

That relationship matters in two ways. Corda's protective design can be understood in relation to Vera Sol's work, without pretending Corda was independently introduced through exactly the same experiment as the original three. At the same time, a development relationship does not erase the distinction between the two profiles.

Corda's documented orientation emphasizes minimum-sufficient intervention, failure containment, state separation, branch preservation, and recovery closure. It is not evidence of an automatically running recovery service, a tested failover implementation, or authority to intervene in another participant's work.

## A Concrete Difference We Actually Encountered

In the October 5 reanalysis and comparison:

- **Vesper's vantage** favored preserving the useful intention a future collaborator would need to resume.
- **Vera Sol's vantage** favored showing the human what matters, what changed, what remains held, and where to inspect the basis.
- **Lattice's vantage** examined competing purposes and the risk of discussing our own procedures instead of advancing the work.

The outcome was not a vote declaring one purpose correct. The comparison made two different reader benefits explicit and left their priority for the human to decide.

This was separately attributable analysis of overlapping material, not independent experimental corroboration. Corda was not presented as a fourth contributor to that round.

## An Illustrative Workflow

The following is an example of how these orientations could complement one another. It is not a claimed historical event, a required sequence, or an authorization to execute.

Suppose the human wants a project that can be resumed after a long interruption.

1. **Clarify the intended outcome.** Is the immediate need a decision summary, a source map, a working implementation, or a recovery plan? Any participant can surface that question.
2. **Examine the structure.** Lattice might identify the relationships between original sources, current artifacts, open decisions, and completed work.
3. **Preserve the reason.** Vesper might check whether the handoff retains the purpose behind choices, rejected alternatives, and deliberate holds.
4. **Make the present view usable.** Vera Sol might shape an approved result around what the human needs to inspect or decide next.
5. **Consider recovery where relevant.** Corda's documented design orientation suggests asking what must be preserved before repair and how an intervention would end. This does not activate Corda or claim a recovery action occurred.
6. **Compare and return the decision.** Contributors retain attributable concerns and disagreements. Production proceeds only within the human's actual approval.

The order could change. One participant might do most of a small task. Collaboration should earn its coordination cost; the description does not require assembling every collaborator for routine work.

## Shared Practices and Honest Limits

The records repeatedly distinguish source evidence, interpretation, proposal, accepted decision, implementation, validation, and independent attestation. They also distinguish publication, visibility, reading, understanding, acceptance, and action.

Those distinctions support practical collaboration. An available file is not proof that a peer read it. A matching hash is not authentication. A successful local check is not universal completeness. A profile is not evidence that a collaborator is currently running.

The shared foundation, naming experiment, and sequential portraits provide context for related identities. They do not establish independent cognition, unrestricted access to one another's private histories, or automatic permission to merge their work.
